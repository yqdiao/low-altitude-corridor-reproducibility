# Provenance and validation record

## Sources and scope

- Source: the user-provided Overleaf project *When Does Congestion Justify Access Charges? Pricing, Auctions, and Capacity Release in Low-Altitude Logistics Corridors*, accessed on September 30, 2026.
- Relevant material was read from `sections/paper_body.tex`, `sections/paper_appendix.tex`, and `sections/managerial_conclusion.tex`.
- The source project contained figures `fig1_revenue_ranking.pdf` through `fig8_regime_maps.pdf`; no Python, R, or MATLAB plotting source was observed.
- Parameters come directly from Table 2 of the paper (LaTeX label `tab:calibration`), figure descriptions in the main text, and axes in the original figures. Stable labels for model equations appear in the figure guide and code comments.
- The Python code is an independent reconstruction. Grid sizes, file organization, 300 dpi PNG output, CSV format, and plot layout are choices made for this reproduction; they are not claimed to be recovered from the original plotting program.

## Archive of original figures

The PDFs in `reference_figures/` were extracted from eight Form XObjects in the compiled paper PDF. They preserve vector curves, fonts, and figure text. They are not screenshots, and the reproduction data are not generated by tracing figure images. The independent PDF wrappers may differ byte-for-byte from the original individual Overleaf figure files.

`reference_figures/manifest.json` records the compiled PDF's SHA-256, corresponding page numbers, and each extracted figure's SHA-256. The full original paper and its Overleaf edit-access link are not included in this public repository. `scripts/extract_reference_figures.py` can repeat the extraction on a compiled PDF with the same structure.

## Automated validation

### Mathematical and numerical checks: all ten pass

1. Key Model A and MUPA numbers for α=1, 1.5, 2, and 3 match the paper's welfare-decomposition table to three decimal places.
2. Model A interior and exit solutions agree with an independent constrained numerical optimizer.
3. MUPA allocation agrees with optimization of a unilateral quantity deviation under the rival's bid and satisfies the welfare accounting identity.
4. Piecewise VCG revenue agrees with externality payments calculated directly and is continuous at the switching point.
5. Figure 4's coordination-threshold endpoints, binding operator, and one-shot test agree with the paper.
6. Figure 5's R=4 intersection and the coordinated-capacity sustainability checks pass across the full range.
7. The positive root for Model D release agrees with direct objective-function optimization; the γ=0 limit and KKT conditions are checked.
8. Figure 7's investment values, envelope residual, and decomposition of the two marginal benefits agree with the paper.
9. Model D's one-dimensional envelope solution agrees with direct joint optimization of `(K,N_L,N_H)`.
10. The analytical maximum payoff from a one-shot zero-price deviation agrees with a dense quantity-grid calculation.

Run `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`. Representative values are in [numerical_validation.json](numerical_validation.json).

### Independent comparison with original curves

`scripts/audit_reference_curves.py` reads path vertices directly from the original figure PDFs, applies a linear coordinate transformation based on the original axis ticks, and evaluates the reconstructed formulas at the same horizontal coordinates.

Twelve original curve segments across Figures 1, 3, 4, 5, and 6 pass this check. The largest absolute deviation is about 3.7×10⁻⁶, including PDF coordinate storage and transformation error; see [reference_curve_audit.json](reference_curve_audit.json). This is neither a comparison of new plots against their own code nor a visual similarity judgment.

Figures 2, 7, and 8 were checked using numerical-model tests, boundary and representative values in the paper, and visual inspection. No vertex-by-vertex comparison is claimed for these multi-panel figures. All regenerated figures were produced and visually inspected.

### Scope of reproducibility

With fixed dependencies, fonts, and platform, the inputs, CSV data, and algorithms are deterministic. Creation and modification timestamps have been removed from the PDFs. Font rendering and underlying libraries can cause image or PDF hashes to differ across operating systems. Compare CSV values and mathematical validation results first; cross-platform byte-for-byte file identity is not the criterion for reproducing the economic model.

## Extrapolations and assumption boundaries in the original figures

1. Figure 1 extends the non-strategic interior revenue formula into N<1, where the actual allocation has reached a single-operator corner. The plotted curve is retained to match the original, while the CSV also provides the correct piecewise values. The dotted MUPA segment at N≤.5 is likewise outside the two-operator positive-price equilibrium.
2. In Figure 2(b), the dotted segment at N≤2.5 is only an extension of the supply-candidate formula, not a solved equilibrium outside its valid domain.
3. Figure 8(b) scans Δ to 10 with mean cost t̄=4. Because t₁=4−Δ/2, Δ≥8 violates t₁>0. The data preserve the original range and add `primitive_cost_valid`. The region classification is an algebraic check under the original plotting formulas and does not remove this primitive-cost assumption limit.
4. The slope-threshold comparisons in Figure 8(a) still require the corresponding model's interior conditions. The original display boundary y=κ/b=0 does not satisfy the assumed κ>0.
5. Figure 5's reserve-price capacity comparison has additional implementation assumptions. Failure of the one-shot construction in Figure 8 does not rule out other forms of zero-price equilibrium.

These notes do not change the original paper; they make the boundaries among the formulas, original figures, and economic interpretations inspectable in the reproduction materials.
